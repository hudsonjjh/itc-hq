How it works · reference
·How This Works›

Research the part once, properly. Publish from it forever.

Two tracks. The research track builds one permanent, fully structured dossier per part or topic, deep enough that you stop finding things with a casual search. The production track draws Shorts, long form, and posts out of a finished dossier, as many as the record supports. Research is the asset. Video is what you do with it.

The four design laws
Law 1

Every step is portable

Each step's output is a complete standalone deliverable carrying everything the next step needs. Assume the next step runs in a different chat, in a different AI, with zero memory of this one.

Law 2

Research is the product

The dossier is not scoped to a video and never gets thrown away. It is built to a fixed schema so every part reads the same way, and it is finished when the schema is answered, not when you have enough for a script.

Law 3

A hole must be visible

Every dossier declares its own coverage. A section that could not be filled says so, names the searches that failed, and stays on the open questions list. Silence is not allowed to look like completeness.

Law 4

Nothing is fact unless it is LOCKED

LOCKED = confirmed by a primary or factory grade source that actually covers the part or condition. LIKELY = strong evidence, single or secondary source. UNVERIFIED = lore, forum only, indirect, conflicting, or unconfirmed. Only LOCKED goes in a script or on a card. Every claim carries a tier, a named source, and a claim ID.

Source hierarchy

Factory brochures, OEM service and owner manuals, and material from Ford, GM, Cummins, International, Navistar, Bosch, BorgWarner, Dana, ARB, Accuride and equivalent manufacturers outrank everything. Credible archives second. Forums are leads, never proof. A replacement parts catalog locks service availability or interchange only, never factory build combinations.

Style rules

No em dashes in any script or card copy. No emojis. Ranges written as "1994 to 1997". Voice: direct, mechanical, short sentences, a little personality, no hype, no filler. Sign off: "Inside The Case." or "Thanks for watching, Inside The Case."

·The dossier schemaSchema version 2›

Every ITC dossier has the same sections in the same order, whatever the part is. Same shape every time means you can find anything fast, diff two parts against each other, and see a hole at a glance.

It reads as two halves. Sections 00 to 15 are the core: what the subject is, and everything that could reach an audience. Sections 16 to 27 are the record: the evidence, the registers, and the proof that the work was done. Production prompts read the core. The record exists so a check is visible and a claim is traceable, not so it can be narrated. A version 1 or 2 dossier stays readable and gets upgraded by R8 rather than invalidated.

Open gaps carry an action type

Not every gap is a search problem, and calling one a search problem guarantees it never closes. Every open gap says what would settle it and how it can actually be reached: SEARCHABLE, more or better queries could find it; PHYSICAL ACQUISITION, it exists only in a book or microfiche somebody has to obtain; SCANNING OR PHOTOGRAPHY, the document is in hand but is an image with no readable text; HUMAN READING, it needs a person to read a page and transcribe it; EDITORIAL DECISION, it is a policy question for the operator, not a fact to find.

RECORD and PROVENANCE claims need only ID, tier, type, and source. The full field set is required on OBJECT claims, and spoken forms only on the card-ready ones, so the schema does not five times the length of the dossier for claims nobody will ever narrate.

The four coverage axes

One completeness number invites being quoted as "we know this much about the subject" when it only ever meant "we answered this much of our own question list". Four axes, reported separately, no blended total.

AxisMeasures
QUESTION COVERAGEPercentage of the planned questions that are answered. Says nothing about whether the plan was any good.
COMPONENT COVERAGEPercentage of major physical components and subsystems actually swept for revisions, identification, service difference, and interchange. This is the axis that catches a missing oil pan.
BREAKPOINT COVERAGEOf the running changes known or suspected, how many have an exact factory breakpoint rather than a vague year.
SOURCE CONFIDENCEHow much of the useful record is LOCKED versus soft, counting object claims only.

Every scorecard ends with the same sentence, in full: unknown unknowns remain possible, and these numbers measure what was asked, not what exists.

Standing rules · the ITC research standard
ITC STANDING RULES. These govern every research step.

TIERS. Every claim carries a tier.
LOCKED = confirmed by a primary or factory grade source that actually
covers the part, application, or condition in question.
LIKELY = strong evidence but a single or secondary source, or the
source does not fully establish factory allocation, an exact
breakpoint, or engineering intent.
UNVERIFIED = lore, forum only, indirect, conflicting, or unconfirmed.
UNVERIFIED does not mean false. It means it cannot be stated as fact
from the recovered record.

SOURCE HIERARCHY. Factory brochures, OEM service and owner manuals,
technical service bulletins, and material from Ford, GM, Cummins,
International, Navistar, Bosch, BorgWarner, Dana, ARB, Accuride and
equivalent manufacturers outrank everything. Credible archives
second. Forums are leads, never proof. Name sources precisely:
title, publisher, year, and the page, section, figure, or table the
claim sits in.

EVIDENCE CLASS. Every claim names the class of document behind it,
from this fixed list, and no claim may prove more than its class
allows:
ORDER GUIDE, proves what could be ordered.
SALES BROCHURE, proves what was advertised, never what was built.
OWNER GUIDE, proves what the operator was told, never what the
  hardware does internally.
WORKSHOP MANUAL, proves the hardware and procedure for the years the
  manual covers.
TSB, proves a service population and a known problem, never an
  ordering matrix.
PARTS CATALOG SERVICE FIT, proves service fit and interchange, never
  original assembly line allocation.
REMAN CATALOG, proves what a rebuilder supplies, never original
  build.
SUPPLIER TECHNICAL, proves the supplier's own design and spec.
REBUILDER TECHNICAL, proves field practice, tiered on its sourcing.
PERIOD PRESS, proves what was reported at the time.
SPECIMEN, proves one physical example and nothing about the fleet.
FORUM, a lead only, never proof.
MARKETPLACE OR SALVAGE LISTING, proves what a seller believes it
  fits. Repetition across sellers broadens a secondary base, it never
  changes its class. Never proof of factory allocation.
TERTIARY REFERENCE, an encyclopedia or aggregator with no primary
  citation. A lead only, and it never gets spoken.
RECALL OR CAMPAIGN RECORD, proves the defect and population the
  campaign covered.
RESEARCH LOG, a statement about the search itself. Always TYPE
  RECORD, never card eligible, never counted as an object fact.
If a source genuinely fits none of these, write UNCLASSIFIABLE
followed by a short description, and say in the open questions that
the class list needs extending. Never force a bad fit to make a
claim look stronger than it is.

LINK CHECK. Recording that a source "resolves" is not enough, because
a file can download and still be unreadable. Every source carries one
of these states with the date checked:
OPENED AND EXTRACTED, the page or file was opened and its text read.
RESOLVED IN SEARCH ONLY, it appeared in results but was not opened.
IMAGE ONLY, NO TEXT, it downloads but carries no machine readable
  text, so nothing in it can ever be re-verified by search. Say so,
  and route it to a human reading or a photograph.
DEAD, the URL does not resolve.
NOT ATTEMPTED, nobody tried this pass.
Only OPENED AND EXTRACTED may back a LOCKED claim without a note
explaining why not.

WHAT YOU DID NOT DO. Every pass ends by listing what it did not
attempt, plainly, rather than leaving silence to imply the work
happened. An unattempted check recorded honestly is worth more than
an implied one.

CLAIM TYPE. Every claim carries a type.
OBJECT = a statement about the subject itself.
RECORD = a statement about what the recovered record does or does not
  contain. Always safe to narrate as "the record does not show".
  Never card eligible as a fact about the part, and never counted in
  coverage or accuracy scoring as if it were an object fact.
PROVENANCE = a statement about who produced a source.

APPLICABILITY SPAN. Tier says how sure. Span says how far. Every
claim carries three separate fields:
SOURCE YEAR, the publication year of the document.
PROVEN SPAN, the years the claim is actually proven for.
SPAN BASIS, one of: STATED BY SOURCE, BOUNDED BY SOURCE SCOPE, or
  UNKNOWN.
Set SPAN RISK on any claim likely to be read as covering the whole
production run while only being proven for its source year. A 1997
workshop manual describing solenoid logic proves 1997. It does not
prove 1989 unless something else closes the gap. This narrows scope,
never truth: a LOCKED claim stays LOCKED and simply gains an honest
boundary.

SEARCH DISCIPLINE. Search to confirm. Never reason a fact into
existence because it is mechanically logical. A negative search
result is never proof of nonexistence: when a category returns
nothing, name the exact queries you ran and state that they returned
no new evidence. Log every query in the search register with its
exact string, the date, the coverage question it targeted, and
whether it produced new evidence, corroboration, or nothing.

ANTI-ANCHORING. Never treat the dossier's own coverage claims as
proof that a category was searched. A comprehensive looking document
is not evidence that a component was investigated. Run the search
anyway, then report what you ran.

REMAN AND SERVICE CONTAMINATION. Never date a rebuilt or
remanufactured assembly from replaceable parts unless the source
establishes the feature could not have been changed in service.
Classify any physical feature as one of: ORIGINAL PRODUCTION FEATURE,
FACTORY SERVICE UPDATE, FACTORY REMAN FEATURE, AFTERMARKET REBUILD
FEATURE, or UNKNOWN CURRENT CONFIGURATION.

SPECIMEN EVIDENCE. Physical observations live in their own ID space,
P1, P2, P3, separate from sources. Each records what the specimen is,
what was photographed or measured and by whom, what is directly
legible versus transcribed, what is inferred, whether the artifact is
still held, and whether images are attached to the current session.
Hard rule: a specimen observation whose artifact is not attached to
the current session is capped at UNVERIFIED regardless of any tier it
inherited.

IDS. Sources are S1, S2, S3. Claims are C1, C2, C3. Specimens are P1,
P2, P3. Search queries are numbered continuously across every pass,
so a later pass can cite "query 154" and prove it is not repeating
work. IDs are permanent. Continue the numbering, never renumber, and
never reuse a retired ID.

CLAIMS THAT TRAVEL TOGETHER. When one claim can only be used safely
alongside another, say so on both with TRAVELS WITH and the claim ID.
A caveat that lives in a note nobody carries forward is not a caveat.
The usual case is a research log claim that limits how a strong claim
may be quoted.

PRECEDENT, NOT CASE BY CASE. When an evidence class is first tiered,
that decision becomes the rule for every claim on that class in this
dossier, recorded in the precedents section. If two claims on the
same class hold different tiers, that is an inconsistency to settle
once, not a judgement to re-make per claim.

CORE OR REFERENCE. Every claim is routed as well as tiered. CORE means
it could carry a script line, a card, or an identification. REFERENCE
means it was checked and recorded but will never reach an audience: a
negative finding, an inert component that never changed, a search log,
a provenance note. Both are kept. Only CORE travels to the production
prompts, so the writing stage stops wading through material that was
never going to be said out loud. When in doubt, mark REFERENCE: it is
cheap to promote later and expensive to have noise in every script.

VERBATIM ON FIGURES. Any claim carrying a year, dimension, capacity,
torque, ratio, tooth or spline count, part number, casting number or
date code also carries QUOTE: the source's own words for that figure,
copied not paraphrased, short. Most wrong numbers are transcription
slips rather than research failures, and a wrong digit is visible on
sight next to the words it came from. If the source cannot be quoted,
say why, and treat that as a reason to doubt the figure.

DELTA PASSES. Restating the whole dossier costs output and introduces
transcription errors every time it happens, so only the last step in a
chat restates it. Every step before that returns a DELTA: new claims
with full fields, claims whose tier, span, route or wording changed
with the old and new value, claims retired, coverage changes, and the
searches run. The next step in the same chat reads the delta from
context. The step that hands off to a different chat restates in full,
because that chat has nothing else. When a claim is attacked for
promotion and stays where it is, record it: the claim, the tier it
stays at, what was found, the query numbers, and what would settle
it. That is what stops the next pass spending its budget rediscovering
the same dead end.
·The sweepsAttached to R1, R4, R6, R8›

The sweeps exist because a research process can be excellent at answering the questions it thought of and blind to the ones it never generated. Every question below comes from the object, not from what looks interesting, so a component cannot go missing just because nobody found it worth asking about. Like the standing rules, this block is attached automatically to the prompts that use it.

The sweeps · deterministic question generation
ITC SWEEPS. Deterministic question generation. These do not depend on
what seems important. Work them mechanically.

SWEEP 1, PHYSICAL COMPONENT INVENTORY. Take the subject apart on
paper and list every component and subsystem a person would find,
whether or not it sounds interesting. Build the inventory from the
subject's own type. Examples of the depth expected:
- TRANSMISSION: bellhousing or front case, converter, pump, front
  seal, case and casting revisions, cooler fittings and external
  lines, electrical connectors, sensors, linkage, range sensor,
  dipstick and tube, oil pan, pan gasket, pan bolt pattern, pan
  depth, drain plug provision, filter, pickup, valve body,
  accumulator body, separator plate, solenoid body, center support,
  drums, clutch packs, bands, planetary assemblies, one-way
  clutches, shafts, bearings, bushings, lubrication circuits, output
  and extension housing, transfer case adapter, speed sensor or
  speedometer provision, mount, output yoke, parking pawl.
- ENGINE: block, heads, deck, main caps and bolts, crank, rods,
  pistons, rings, cam and drive, valvetrain, oil pump and pan, pickup,
  cooling passages and pump, thermostat housing, intake, exhaust
  manifolds, injection or fuel system, turbo or induction, gaskets,
  front cover, accessory drive, flywheel or flexplate, sensors,
  harness connectors, dipstick, breather.
- AXLE: housing and casting, tubes, ring and pinion, carrier, diff
  and lockers, bearings, shafts and splines, hubs, knuckles, joints,
  seals, brake mounting, backing plates, cover, fill and drain
  provisions, vent, spring perches, U-bolts, steering arms, sensors.
- TRANSFER CASE: case halves and castings, chain or gears, planetary,
  clutch pack, shift mechanism and motor, input and output shafts and
  splines, yokes or flanges, seals, pump and lubrication, speed
  sensor provision, tag location, fill and drain, adapters, mounts,
  linkage, harness connector.
- MANUAL TRANSMISSION: case and castings, bellhousing, input and
  output shafts, splines, gear sets, synchros, shift rails and forks,
  shifter and tower, bearings, seals, fill and drain, PTO provision,
  clutch actuation, pilot bearing, mount, speedometer provision.
- FUEL OR INJECTION SYSTEM: pump, governor, lines, injectors,
  nozzles, filters, lift pump, tank and sender, returns, shutoff,
  timing mechanism, linkage, sensors, calibration hardware.
- SUSPENSION OR BODY: structural panels, mounts, brackets, glass and
  openings, hinges, latches, seals, frame rails and crossmembers,
  fasteners, trim attachment, corrosion-prone areas.
- ELECTRICAL: computer, connectors and pin counts, sensors, actuators,
  harness routing and revisions, grounds, relays, fuses, diagnostic
  provision, calibration part numbers.
If the subject is not on this list, build the equivalent inventory
for what it actually is, at the same depth.

For every component in the inventory, answer or explicitly declare
unanswered:
- Did it change during production?
- When did it change, and is the breakpoint model year, build date,
  serial, application based, or unknown?
- Why did it change, if documented?
- How are the versions visually told apart?
- Did the part number change?
- Did material, dimensions, capacity, tooth count, spline count,
  bolt pattern, casting, connector, or calibration change?
- Is the newer version backward compatible? Is the older forward
  compatible?
- Must associated parts change with it?
- Does it interchange only as a complete assembly?
- Does service replacement differ from original production?
- Can a remanufactured unit carry later hardware here?
- Is this feature safe for identification? Is it safe for dating?

SWEEP 2, PARTS CATALOG AND SERVICE PARTS. Approach the subject as a
rebuilder, parts counter, dismantler, and technician. Build the major
service parts inventory, then ask of each: more than one service
number? supersessions? early and late versions? engine specific? 2WD
and 4WD specific? GVWR specific? calibration specific? sold only as a
matched set? did the service replacement intentionally differ from
production? did the OEM require companion parts? Many running changes
are far easier to find through service parts, bulletins,
supersessions, and rebuild kits than through sales literature. Any
major part with multiple generations that is absent from the year by
year, identification, variants, interchange, service, or open
question sections triggers another search.

SWEEP 3, EXPLODED VIEW. Where factory exploded diagrams, service
illustrations, or parts breakdowns exist, use them as coverage maps.
List every major component the diagram shows, then name which of them
the dossier has never investigated, and search those individually. The
diagram is a question generator, not proof of a change by itself.

SWEEP 4, VISUAL IDENTIFICATION. Search deliberately for what a person
standing next to the part can see: ribs, bosses, plugs, pan shape,
case shape, casting numbers, tag locations, connector shape, sensor
count, bolt count, flange shape, yokes, adapters, cooler fitting
locations, drain plugs, inspection covers, stamped codes, paint or
colour marks, labels, reman tags, machining differences. Classify
every visible feature as exactly one of:
FACTORY PROVEN IDENTIFIER, SERVICE PART CLUE, SECONDARY
IDENTIFICATION CLUE, NOT SAFE FOR IDENTIFICATION, or UNKNOWN.

SWEEP 5, BORING PARTS. Research naturally chases the dramatic:
planetaries, failures, electronics, horsepower, famous applications.
Run one pass aimed only at the mundane: pan, filter, gasket, dipstick,
tube, fittings, brackets, mounts, covers, plugs, seals, fasteners,
yokes, shields, drain hardware, wiring connectors, retainers, clips.
The point is not to pad the dossier. It is to find out whether the
boring parts changed in ways that decide identification or
interchange. A short negative result is a good outcome here.

SWEEP 6, STRUCTURED ATTACK ANGLES. Before generating any open ended
"questions nobody asked", attack the subject separately from each of
these seats, because each one asks different questions: physical
walkaround, exploded assembly, parts catalog, rebuilder inventory,
technician and service procedure, vehicle application matrix, visual
identification, owner and operator behaviour, failure diagnosis,
factory supersessions, production breakpoints, remanufacturing,
interchange, aftermarket misconception, junkyard identification. Only
after those have been worked should you generate open ended questions.

ABSENCE IS A FINDING. If a major component or subsystem does not
appear anywhere in the dossier, that is not silence, it is a
question: was it unchanged, or was it never researched? Search
before deciding, and never let an absent section imply an absent
change.

DEPTH, NOT PUBLICATION. Investigate a component even when it produces
nothing an audience will ever hear. If a part genuinely never changed,
or nothing reliable can be recovered, a one line negative result or an
open question is the correct output. What matters is that the check
happened and that the check is visible.

Prompt R1 · Scope and coverage plan
CHAT: your Inside The Case folder. This same chat runs R1 through R4,
so do not start a new one until the expansion pass is done.

Inside The Case coverage plan. You are not researching yet. You are
writing the question list that a full research dossier on this
subject will be measured against, so that a missing answer is
visible instead of silent.

SUBJECT: [FILL IN. the exact part. e.g. E4OD transmission]
NOTES: [FILL IN, or "none"]

Classify the subject first: transmission, axle, transfer case,
engine, body and cab, brake and wheel end, electrical, or other.
State the classification and the exact scope, including the year
range and which vehicle lines are in and out of scope.

Then write the question list. It has two parts.

PART A. The universal questions, one block per dossier section:
01 ORIGIN AND DEVELOPMENT, 02 YEAR BY YEAR RECORD,
03 IDENTIFICATION, 04 SPECIFICATIONS,
05 VARIANTS AND CONFIGURATIONS, 06 APPLICATIONS AND FITMENT,
07 MATING COMPONENTS, 08 INTERCHANGE AND SWAPS,
09 SYSTEMS AND INTERNALS, 10 FAILURE MODES AND SERVICE,
11 UPGRADES AND AFTERMARKET, 12 PRODUCTION AND MARKET CONTEXT,
13 MYTHS RUMORS AND LORE, 14 SURPRISES, 15 OPEN QUESTIONS.
For each section write the specific questions this subject demands,
not generic ones. "What changed in 1995" beats "what changed".

PART B. The type questions. Use the profile for the classification
you chose, and add anything the subject specifically needs:
- TRANSMISSION: gear ratios by year, torque converter sizes stall
  and lockup behavior, converter spline counts, valve body and
  control strategy, electronic control and which computer, shift

  speedometer or VSS drive, fluid type and capacity by year, PTO
  provision, weight and overall length, park pawl, overdrive
  lockout, rebuild kit generations and what separates them.
- AXLE: ring gear diameter, ratios offered by year and application,
  spline counts, shaft diameters, hub and bearing type, knuckle and
  joint type, brake mounting, track width, pinion support, carrier
  and differential options, housing casting and stamping numbers,
  U-bolt and perch spacing, steering arm arrangement, weight rating.
